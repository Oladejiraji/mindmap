# Mindmap
## Enterprise Research Workflow Example

# Scenario

A large technology company is evaluating whether to launch a new AI powered collaborative presentation feature inside its productivity suite.

The initiative involves:

- Product Management
- Design
- Engineering
- Research
- Applied AI teams
- Legal
- Trust and Safety
- Go To Market teams

The company currently uses:

- Linear for execution tracking
- Slack for communication
- Google Docs for research
- Figma for design
- Internal AI tools for exploration

The problem is that research becomes fragmented across:

- disconnected documents
- Slack threads
- meeting notes
- tickets
- prototype files
- duplicated investigations
- inconsistent conclusions

Mindmap becomes the collaborative research layer that sits before production execution.

Linear tracks execution.
Mindmap tracks reasoning.

---

# User Story

## Primary User

Maya
Senior Product Manager
AI Productivity Team

Maya is responsible for evaluating whether the company should ship an AI assisted collaborative presentation feature.

The feature would allow:

- AI generated slide structures
- collaborative brainstorming
- contextual summarization
- presentation synthesis from documents and meetings

The decision affects multiple organizations and requires several weeks of coordinated research.

Maya needs a system where:

- teams can investigate independently
- findings remain connected
- reasoning is visible
- research does not become fragmented
- leadership can understand progress
- conclusions remain traceable

---

# Phase 1
## Research Workspace Creation

Maya creates a new Mindmap workspace.

Workspace title:

"AI Collaborative Presentations"

She invites:

- Product Managers
- Designers
- Frontend Engineers
- ML Engineers
- User Researchers
- Legal reviewers
- GTM stakeholders

The root node is:

"Should We Build AI Collaborative Presentations?"

The node contains:

- initial problem framing
- market context
- company goals
- leadership requirements
- attached strategy docs
- links to relevant Linear initiatives

---

# Phase 2
## Investigation Branching

The team begins splitting the problem into independent research branches.

New branches emerge:

- Competitive Analysis
- User Research
- Technical Feasibility
- AI Infrastructure Cost
- Enterprise Security
- Legal Review
- Presentation UX Exploration
- Internal Dogfooding
- Monetization Strategy
- Go To Market Risks

Each branch becomes an isolated research environment with inherited context from the parent investigation.

Teams work in parallel.

---

# Phase 3
## Cross Functional Collaboration

## Competitive Analysis Branch

The Product Marketing team investigates:

- Microsoft Copilot
- Canva AI
- Gamma
- Notion AI
- Google Workspace AI

They attach:

- screenshots
- pricing comparisons
- positioning summaries
- feature matrices
- launch timelines

AI models summarize:

- competitive gaps
- strategic opportunities
- market saturation
- differentiation patterns

---

## User Research Branch

UX Researchers upload:

- interview transcripts
- survey results
- usability studies
- customer pain points

Designers branch further into:

- collaborative editing concepts
- AI assisted workflows
- slide generation patterns
- multiplayer presentation structures

Researchers synthesize findings into:

- top requested capabilities
- highest friction workflows
- enterprise adoption concerns

---

## Technical Feasibility Branch

Engineering teams investigate:

- inference costs
- streaming collaboration architecture
- latency constraints
- vector storage
- document ingestion
- permissions systems
- realtime synchronization

ML engineers create sub branches for:

- multimodal understanding
- layout generation
- slide summarization
- contextual retrieval

Different AI models are used across nodes depending on the task.

---

## Legal And Trust Branch

Legal and Trust teams investigate:

- copyrighted training data concerns
- enterprise compliance
- hallucination risk
- generated content ownership
- regional policy constraints
- customer data isolation

This branch remains partially restricted to approved stakeholders.

---

# Phase 4
## Realtime Collaboration

The workspace becomes highly active.

Users can see:

- which investigations are active
- who is researching where
- which branches are unresolved
- which nodes recently updated
- where AI synthesis is running

Examples:

- A designer is actively exploring onboarding UX
- A researcher is synthesizing interview transcripts
- An ML engineer is benchmarking inference latency
- Legal is reviewing enterprise compliance requirements

The graph itself communicates organizational activity.

Leadership can zoom out and understand:

- investigation density
- unresolved risks
- active collaboration areas
- emerging consensus

Without reading every document.

---

# Phase 5
## Synthesis

After several weeks, synthesis nodes begin appearing.

Major synthesis nodes include:

- User Value Summary
- Technical Feasibility Summary
- Competitive Positioning Summary
- Enterprise Risk Summary
- Revenue Opportunity Summary

Each synthesis node aggregates findings from multiple branches.

The synthesis process includes:

- AI generated summaries
- human review
- evidence comparison
- contradiction resolution
- citation linking
- recommendation drafting

Leadership stakeholders review synthesis nodes rather than raw investigations.

---

# Phase 6
## Decision Review

An executive review node is created.

The node contains:

- synthesized findings
- recommended scope
- launch risks
- engineering estimates
- user demand evidence
- competitive analysis
- monetization opportunities
- rollout recommendations

Executives can visually trace:

- where conclusions originated
- which teams contributed
- unresolved concerns
- supporting evidence
- alternative recommendations

The reasoning process is fully visible.

---

# Phase 7
## Transition To Execution

Once approved, the initiative transitions into production execution.

Mindmap remains the research and reasoning layer.

Linear becomes the execution layer.

The final synthesis node links directly to:

- implementation epics
- engineering milestones
- rollout phases
- experiment tracking
- launch planning

Execution teams can always reference:

- original reasoning
- research lineage
- technical tradeoffs
- user evidence
- rejected alternatives

The graph remains persistent organizational memory.
